# Confidence rules

Confidence answers how safe the interpretation is, not how impressive it sounds.

- `high`: direct, internally consistent source support; scope, ownership, and metric context are clear.
- `medium`: support is credible but one material detail, dependency, or comparison is incomplete.
- `low`: self-report only, conflicting sources, unclear scope, or speculative interpretation.

Confidence never upgrades `unverified` to `verified`. Contradictions lower confidence and become open questions. Use the weakest material component to calibrate the overall claim: a verified result with unclear ownership cannot receive high confidence as an individual achievement.
