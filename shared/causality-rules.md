# Causality rules

Classify causal support separately from evidence status and attribution.

- `supported`: intervention, sequence, comparison, and mechanism are documented; major confounders are addressed.
- `plausible`: mechanism and timing fit, but isolation is incomplete.
- `correlated`: the action and outcome moved together without enough evidence for causation.
- `unknown`: information is insufficient.
- `not_applicable`: the claim does not assert an outcome relationship.

Check pricing, promotions, seasonality, inventory availability, product-mix changes, platform policy, competitor behavior, exchange rates, reporting windows, and other teams. Use causal verbs only for `supported`; prefer “contributed to” for `plausible` and neutral coexistence language for `correlated`.

A strong metric does not repair a weak causal link. A lower advertising efficiency ratio, for example, does not by itself prove stronger profit, sales, or organic performance.
