# Experienced-hire level calibration

Use this reference for every experienced-hire resume. Years are a routing clue, not proof of seniority.

## Two-step classification

First identify the target JD's market band: `1-3`, `3-5`, or `5-10`. Then classify demonstrated scope independently:

- `operator`: independently executes a product, listing, account, or campaign loop;
- `senior_ic`: owns a product line, category, or repeatable operating mechanism without mandatory people management;
- `manager`: owns targets through a team, including allocation, coaching, review, and accountability;
- `business_owner`: owns multi-market or portfolio strategy, P&L-like trade-offs, resources, and organizational mechanisms.

Report one of three calibration states:

- `aligned`: evidence already matches the target archetype;
- `stretch`: some next-level evidence exists, but gaps must be named;
- `title_inflated`: title or tenure is higher than demonstrated responsibility; position by actual scope.

## What stays common

Across all experienced bands, current postings commonly expect platform rule awareness, account health, Listing and traffic work, advertising, inventory, data analysis, English reading/writing, execution, and cross-functional communication. Do not repeat all of these as generic skill labels. Prove them at the depth expected for the target band.

## What changes by band

| Dimension | 1–3 years | 3–5 years | 5–10 years |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Typical identity | independent operator / product operator | senior operator / category owner / team lead | operations manager / business owner / principal specialist |
| Main operating unit | links, products, one account or one market | product line, category, portfolio, important account | multi-category, multi-market, brand, department or business unit |
| Decision level | execute and adjust within a known playbook | diagnose, choose strategy, allocate within owned scope | set direction, goals, budgets, portfolio and organizational mechanisms |
| Result proof | complete launch or optimization loop; account health; advertising, conversion, inventory result | repeatable product/category outcomes; profit and inventory trade-offs; multiple-cycle evidence | annual/quarterly target delivery; P&L quality; portfolio health; organizational output |
| Time horizon | daily to monthly | monthly to quarterly and lifecycle | quarterly to annual and multi-year capability |
| Collaboration | completes handoffs and resolves operating exceptions | drives product, creative, advertising, supply or finance collaboration | designs decision cadence, allocates resources, resolves cross-functional conflicts |
| People evidence | not required | split track: no management requirement for senior IC; coaching/leading required only for lead roles | management track requires hiring, goal setting, coaching and performance; principal-IC track requires broad expert influence |
| Resume center | one or two complete closed loops | judgment, mechanisms, scope expansion and repeatability | business planning, resource choices, organization, risk and durable growth |

## Evidence gates

### 1–3 years

Require at least one complete loop the candidate can explain end to end: problem, baseline, owned action, result, and review. “熟悉后台、负责日常运营” is insufficient. Strong evidence includes an independently operated product set, a real launch, a conversion/advertising repair, inventory planning, or an account-risk resolution.

### 3–5 years

Require decisions across multiple levers rather than isolated execution. Look for product-line or category scope, sales-profit-inventory trade-offs, lifecycle thinking, reusable methods, and influence across functions. Split the route:

- **senior IC:** emphasize independent business judgment, portfolio mechanisms, and repeatability;
- **team lead/manager:** additionally require target allocation, review cadence, coaching, and team output.

Do not force team management merely because the candidate has four or five years of experience.

### 5–10 years

Require evidence beyond “personally good at operating a store.” Look for strategy, goal decomposition, resource or budget allocation, P&L-like accountability, multi-market/category decisions, organization building, governance, and sustained outcomes across cycles.

Split the route:

- **manager/business owner:** team design and team-throughput evidence is mandatory;
- **principal specialist:** people management may be absent, but expert influence must span products, markets, mechanisms, or major decisions.

If neither route is supported, do not manufacture leadership. Position the candidate as a strong operator and list the missing next-level proof.

## Positioning output

Produce two versions when useful:

1. **Conservative:** matches demonstrated scope today.
2. **Stretch:** targets the next plausible role using existing evidence; explicitly list what must be confirmed or strengthened.

The stretch version may reframe real scope but may not invent people management, P&L ownership, budgets, markets, or results.
