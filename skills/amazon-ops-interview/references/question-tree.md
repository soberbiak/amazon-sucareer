# Interview question tree

Use the resume's exact claim as the root. Do not ask every branch when one answer already resolves it.

## Universal branches

1. **Scope** — Which marketplace, products, account, period, and business size?
2. **Problem** — What was happening, how did you detect it, and why did it matter?
3. **Baseline** — What was the starting value and comparison basis?
4. **Ownership** — Which decisions and actions were yours? Who else contributed?
5. **Mechanism** — Why should the action affect the result?
6. **Alternatives** — What other options did you consider and reject?
7. **Execution** — What sequence, cadence, thresholds, or tools did you use?
8. **Result** — What changed, over what window, using which denominator?
9. **Attribution** — What else changed at the same time? How did you isolate your effect?
10. **Failure and learning** — What did not work? What would you change?
11. **Transfer** — Under what conditions would the approach fail or generalize?

## Advertising claim branches

- campaign type and targeting scope;
- search-term harvesting and negative rules;
- budget and bid change logic;
- ACoS versus TACoS denominator;
- organic rank, price, coupon, inventory, and seasonality dependencies;
- lag and attribution window;
- marginal return and stopping rule.

## Inventory claim branches

- forecast horizon and granularity;
- lead-time components and variability;
- service-level or stockout trade-off;
- inbound, stranded, reserved, and aging treatment;
- promotion and seasonality assumptions;
- decision threshold and exception handling;
- working-capital or storage-fee consequence.

## Listing or conversion claim branches

- traffic quality versus page conversion;
- parent/child or ASIN scope;
- content, price, review, availability, and competitor changes;
- test design and sample sufficiency;
- mobile versus desktop or market-language considerations;
- actual business outcome versus recommendation only.

## Profitability claim branches

- revenue definition and currency;
- COGS, freight, Amazon fees, ads, refunds, and tax treatment;
- gross margin versus contribution margin;
- FX and mix changes;
- allocation method for shared costs;
- actionability and governance cadence.
